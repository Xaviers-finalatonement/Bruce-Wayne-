### Factual and ethical items

- **F1 — OPEN (blocking): raw-vs-cooked count (≈52.2–55.7 s).**
  - **What's on screen:** raw shows 6 C tiles. Cooked starts with 6, and 4 of them fade out (≈53.3–54.2), leaving 2. That reads as "cooking removes about two-thirds of the vitamin C".
  - **Source gap:** S3 gives no amount ("can reduce"), and `claims.md` Notes say loss amounts were not sourced.
  - **Label:** the graphic carries no "illustration" label.
- **F2 — OPEN (blocking): microwave / steamer / pan trio (≈46.8–51.2 s).**
  - **Overstated:** the C tiles fade one method at a time to blank cream squares, so the shot ends on all vitamin C gone. That overstates "can lower" (C8, C9).
  - **Implied ranking:** while it plays, the microwave and steamer look empty while the pan still has 4 green tiles, which implies an order no source gives (see `claims.md` Notes).
  - **Contradiction:** the trio ends on zero, but the next shot keeps 2 of 6.
- **F3 — OPEN (blocking): "vitamins A · D · E · K" (≈29.2–33.8 s).**
  - **What's on screen:** the label and the A D E K tiles sit beside the spinach-and-broccoli pan while the voice says "in spinach and broccoli". Together they imply these vegetables supply vitamin D.
  - **What's sourced:** C6 sources only E and K in spinach and broccoli. C4 sources only that A, D, E and K are absorbed more easily with fat. No row supports D in vegetables, and the VO itself says only "E and K".
- **F4 — OPEN (hedge wider than source): "raw = best sources" (≈52.2–55.2 s).**
  - **Source:** C10 says "The best food sources **of vitamin C** are uncooked or raw fruits and vegetables."
  - **Problem:** without "vitamin C" the sticker reads as "raw is best" in general. The episode itself shows that is false for carotenoids, and the line drifts toward diet advice.
- **F5 — weak hedge (fix required, not blocking on its own): hook "Heat costs your peppers." (0–2.8 s, VO and title).**
  - **Source:** S3 says cooking "can reduce" vitamin C.
  - **Current justification:** C7 calls the line a headline and says the hedged version comes later.
  - **Rule:** §5 says to hedge as far as the source goes. "Heat can cost your peppers." keeps the hook and matches the source.
- **F6 — paperwork:**
  - **C5 quote:** "packed with vitamin C" is fine only if the quote includes the S3 lead-in, "Vegetables that are the highest sources of vitamin C include…". The current quote shows only the list items. Add the lead-in.
  - **Missing columns:** `claims.md` lacks the per-row "checked by" and "date" columns that §5 requires.
- **F7 — process note for Bruce (not counted as open):** `claims.md` is certified by Claude, not by a person (§5). Bruce should spot-check C3, C4, C8 and C10, or accept an AI check.
- **Spoken lines:** C1–C11 as spoken match their quoted sources and hedges ("a little fat", "more easily", "can lower"). The "ride" picture is framed as a metaphor ("Think of it like a ride").
- **Clean areas:** no diet advice, calorie talk, brands or dosing anywhere. The end card carries "Food education, not diet advice.", and `platform.json` text is hedged.

### Verdict: **FAIL**

1. **Scores:** (h) = 2 is below the floor of 3. (a) = 3 and (g) = 3, where the gate needs ≥ 4.
2. **Facts:** four factual items are open (F1–F4).
