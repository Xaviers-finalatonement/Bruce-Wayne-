# Pan Theory — style sheet (draft name)

**Subject:** food and cooking science. What heat, salt, oil and time really do to your food.
**Promise:** the reason behind the recipe, so your next meal is better.
**Look:** top-down over a warm countertop. Ingredients are matte, slightly chunky 3D shapes. When heat hits, the camera drops into the food and molecules appear.

## Palette

| Name | Hex | Use | Contrast |
|---|---|---|---|
| Countertop | `#F7F1E6` | background | — |
| Cast Iron | `#1F1D1B` | text, pans | 15.0:1 |
| Tomato | `#B53A25` | key word, heat | 5.2:1 |
| Herb | `#3A7235` | secondary labels | 5.1:1 |
| Butter | `#F3C54B` | highlight behind Cast Iron text | Cast Iron on Butter 10.3:1 |

## Fonts

**Rubik** 500/800 for titles; **Nunito** 400/800 for captions. Both OFL and self-hosted.

## Camera

- Overhead by default, like a cooking show.
- Motivated drops: when heat or oil hits, the camera falls straight down into the pan, down to molecule scale.
- Side-by-side "raw vs cooked" comparisons.
- No shake. No orbiting.

## Pacing and voice

- Upbeat-medium. A new beat every 2 s. About 155 words per minute.
- A friendly home cook who knows the chemistry. "Here's what your pan is really doing."
- Framed as education, never as diet or medical advice. Says "about" and "up to".

## Sound

Real-feeling kitchen foley made in code (sizzle, chop, pour) over light acoustic rhythm.

## Signature

Every episode ends on **"The Pan Theory:"** with one line you can use tonight.

## Never

Diet advice, calorie shaming, brand or product shots, glossy food-ad look.
